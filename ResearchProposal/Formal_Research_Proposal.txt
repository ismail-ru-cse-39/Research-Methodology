1.  Abstract, Introduction and Background
1.1  Context and domain framework
A compressed model can now answer on a phone, and the question need not leave the device. The open problem is when that answer should be withheld. This proposal tests one gate beside a frozen 4-bit model on one Android phone. The gate joins the length-adjusted token probability of a single answer to live memory and thermal headroom, then shows the answer or says "I'm not sure." On SQuAD 2.0 and MuSiQue-Full it is compared with always answering and with confidence only, at a wrong-answer rate of at most 20% among shown answers. A gain only under heat or low memory would support the gate. No gain, or a test rate above 20%, is kept and is not refitted.
1.2  The AI landscape
On-device systems still answer every question (Liu et al., 2024; Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). Selective question answering can refuse, but only from model confidence (Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020). Free memory and heat enter neither decision.
1.3  Problem statement
A rule with no model fails twice. Refusing every question answers nothing. Refusing only when the phone is hot misses a confident error on a cool phone, and it blocks a correct answer that is merely slow. The cost is a fluent mistake that stays on the device.
In the question, (I) is the method, (O) the result, (P) the phone, and (C) the comparison. Coverage is the share of questions that receive an answer.
Primary research question. To what extent can a device-aware gate (I) keep the wrong-answer rate among answered questions at or below 20%, while answering as many as possible (O), for a 4-bit small language model whose weights are not changed, on an Android phone (P), compared with always answering and with a gate that uses confidence only (C)?
Sub-RQ 1. How can one answer’s confidence be combined with free memory and heat, without training the model again?
Sub-RQ 2. On the same questions, how do low memory and a long hot run change calibration and coverage at a given error rate?
Sub-RQ 3. At the 20% limit, does the gate answer more under strain than confidence-only, and does that gain disappear when the phone is cool?
2.  Research Aims and Objectives
2.1  Overarching project aim
The aim is to test one post-hoc gate that joins a frozen model’s confidence to live memory and temperature, at the same error limit as a confidence-only gate.
2.2  Measurable project objectives
Six objectives follow. (1) Freeze the comparisons and the reading list before any scored run. (2) Split SQuAD 2.0 and MuSiQue-Full into development and test sets, with no personal data (Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, 2018; Trivedi et al., 2022). (3) Build the Section 4 pipeline. (4) Fit τ0, α, and β on the development questions only. (5) Score risk, coverage, calibration, and phone readings on the test set, including one-signal runs. (6) Review 30–60 errors and unnecessary refusals.
3.  Literature Review
3.1  State-of-the-art thematic synthesis
The sources that matter form two groups, and they do not yet decide the same question. A supplied passage means the answer is not limited to facts stored in the weights (Lewis et al., 2020). Models below one billion parameters can run on a phone, yet the systems built for that setting, including MobileRAG, still answer every time (Liu et al., 2024; Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). Selective prediction takes the opposite decision. It maximises coverage while holding the error among released answers to a chosen limit, and it shows that a raw top probability is too confident when the test questions differ from the training questions (Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020).
3.2  Critical evaluation of existing systems
A stronger uncertainty score, semantic entropy, needs several answers to the same question, and those extra answers would heat the phone, so this study uses one generation (Kuhn, Gal and Farquhar, 2023). Four-bit GPTQ moves confidence most where the model was already unsure (Proskurina et al., 2024). Repair by soft prompts changes the model, is hard to inspect after compression, and needs large new labelled sets (Zhong et al., 2025). This gate takes none of those routes: the weights stay frozen, the labels are the answers already published, and the decision is the logged comparison of C with τ. Phone studies, for their part, treat heat and memory as speed and battery rather than as a reason to refuse. On most non-Apple phones, longer prompts cut speed by more than 10%, and a Snapdragon clock is nearly halved by the ninth repeat (Xiao et al., 2024). Four-bit inference can use more energy than 3-bit, and the hottest phone is not the same in every test (Li et al., 2025). Heat is therefore read live, not treated as a fixed property of 4-bit storage.
3.3  Proposed methodological justification
The gate raises the bar as free memory falls or the room before severe heat shrinks, and it refuses on a low-memory flag or severe heat. Always answering is the non-AI baseline. Confidence-only removes the phone terms. The same 20% limit makes a gain attributable to the phone.
4.  System Design Architecture
4.1  End-to-end system blueprint
Figure 1 separates the answer the model wrote from the decision to show it. The published passage is supplied, so a failed search is not scored as a model error, and live retrieval is left outside this study (Section 8). One generation returns a candidate and its confidence, C. The log is copied off the phone before any score is calculated.

Figure 1. The path from a public question and its passage, through a frozen 4-bit model and the phone’s live readings, to the gate that may refuse. The heavy border marks the new part.
4.2  Architectural sub-systems
The gate sits after a frozen model, so a gain is traced to the decision rather than to new weights. Batches are cool or sustained. A byte buffer lowers free memory without changing the question, and passage length is logged but is not a feature. There is no network call. llama.cpp runs one 4-bit GGUF file (Gerganov, n.d.). The pilot then freezes the larger Qwen2.5 file, 0.5B or 1.5B, that leaves at least 1 GB free. Both sizes have been run on a Galaxy S24; that does not establish the instruction-tuned checkpoint (Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). C is the length-adjusted token probability, the exponential of the mean log probability, not the arithmetic mean.
getThermalHeadroom(0) is the heat now; 1.0 is SEVERE (Android Developers, 2026). Missing headroom maps from NONE to CRITICAL. Memory pressure is 0 at idle and 1 when memory is low. The answer is shown only if C is at least τ, memory is not low, and heat is below SEVERE: τ = τ0 + α (memory pressure) + β (thermal pressure). τ0 is fitted on cool development items for the most answers at or below 20% error. α and β are then fitted on the warm and low-memory runs, with τ0 locked. Confidence-only sets α and β to zero. The test set is never used. The log stores the question id, C, both pressures, τ, and the decision.
5.  Applied Methodology and Implementation
5.1  Data engineering and pipeline strategy
MuSiQue-Full adds items that need two to four steps. Each set gives 200 development and 300 test questions, stratified on answerability, with no question in both. One instruction is added: answer from the passage, or say that it does not. The reading list is frozen first, from the ACL Anthology, PMLR, and arXiv (2017–2025) on on-device models, selective question answering, and calibration after quantization. Cool means one question after a rest. Warm means at least ten minutes back to back.
5.2  Model selection and the tuned rule
No weights are trained, because the question is whether a rule beside the model is enough. Always-answer is the non-AI baseline and skips the gate. Memory-only and temperature-only repeat the same fit with the other coefficient set to zero, so a failure of one signal can be told apart from a failure of the other. τ0, α, and β are the only tuned values, and they are chosen for coverage at the 20% limit rather than by training a network.
5.3  Technical stack and compute
Kotlin reads the sensors, llama.cpp runs the model, and Python scores the logs. Scoring includes expected calibration error (ECE), the gap between stated confidence and the share of answers that are right (Guo et al., 2017). There is no cloud GPU and no network call during an answer. A second phone, if used, is its own tier and is not pooled.
5.4  Quantitative evaluation and expected results
A shown answer is wrong if the item had no answer, or if token F1 is below 0.5. The headline is coverage at a risk of at most 20%, with the median and middle half of time, memory, and headroom, and the full risk–coverage curve. More coverage than confidence-only under strain, and little extra when the phone is cool, would support the gate. No gain means the sensors are not worth reading. A risk above 20% for every method is kept and is not refitted.
6.  Ethical, Legal, and Socio-Economic Considerations
6.1  Regulatory compliance and data governance
Only public, licensed data are used. No names or locations are stored, and questions stay on the phone, so the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is not triggered. The prototype is not a high-risk system under the EU AI Act.
6.2  Algorithmic bias and mitigation
The questions are English and Wikipedia-style, so no fairness claim is made. The audit splits the error rate and the refusal rate by answerability and by passage length.
6.3  Risk, accountability, and explanation
SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) split a prediction across features and are not used: the log stores C, both pressures, and τ. A refusal can spare a paid cloud call and also withholds correct answers. That trade-off is reported as coverage lost.

8.  Strengths and Weaknesses
The passage, the model, and the questions stay fixed. τ0 is locked before α and β, and the test set is used once. A negative result stands. The memory block keeps the wording fixed, and every refusal can be rebuilt from C and τ. One phone cannot stand for every chip (Xiao et al., 2024). Token probability is weaker than semantic entropy, and the stronger score would heat the phone. Passages are supplied, the sets are English only, and 20% is a research choice.
