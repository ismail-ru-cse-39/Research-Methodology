1.  Abstract, Introduction and Background
1.1  Context and domain framework
A small language model can answer on an Android phone without uploading the text (Liu et al., 2024). In retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) the answer is conditioned on a passage, not on the model alone (Lewis et al., 2020). Free memory changes as other apps run, and repeated inference warms the phone until the processor slows.
1.2  The AI landscape
MobileRAG makes on-device retrieval small enough for a Galaxy S24, and it still returns an answer (Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). Selective question answering can withhold a likely error, but from confidence alone (Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020). Neither decision uses live memory or temperature.
1.3  Problem statement
Four-bit quantization shifts confidence most where the model was already unsure, and calibration often worsens (Proskurina et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2025). Sustained runs also slow under frequency scaling, by about 10–20% on longer prompts and by up to 30% across repeats on a Snapdragon phone (Xiao et al., 2024). A fixed cutoff can release a confident error, with no larger model online to correct it.
Primary research question. To what extent can a device-aware abstention gate (I) keep the wrong-answer rate among answered questions at or below 20%, while maximising coverage (O), for a frozen 4-bit small language model on an Android phone (P), compared with always answering and with a confidence-only gate (C)?
Sub-RQ 1. How can one answer's token confidence be combined with available memory and thermal headroom, without retraining?
Sub-RQ 2. On the same questions, how do low memory and sustained heat change calibration and the risk–coverage trade-off?
Sub-RQ 3. At the 20% risk limit, does the device-aware rule answer more questions under strain than the confidence-only rule, and does that gap close when the phone is cool and memory is free?
2.  Research Aims and Objectives
2.1  Overarching project aim
The aim is to test whether a gate beside a frozen quantized model can withhold an Android answer that is too likely to be wrong, using confidence together with live memory and temperature.
2.2  Measurable project objectives
Six objectives follow. (1) Fix the baselines and metrics from the on-device RAG, abstention, and calibration literature before any scored run. (2) Build stratified development and test splits of SQuAD 2.0 and MuSiQue-Full, including unanswerable items and no personal data (Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, 2018; Trivedi et al., 2022). (3) Implement the Section 4 pipeline: frozen inference, telemetry, the gate, and a local logger. (4) Run the always-answer and confidence-only baselines, and fit the gate coefficients on the development split only. (5) Compare selective risk, coverage, calibration, latency, memory, and temperature on the test split, with memory-only and temperature-only ablations. (6) Review 30–60 errors and unnecessary refusals, and state the limits in Sections 6 and 8.
3.  Literature Review
3.1  State-of-the-art thematic synthesis
RAG attaches a passage so the generator is not limited to its weights (Lewis et al., 2020). Server-scale models do not fit on a phone. Sub-billion-parameter models at 4-bit precision do (Liu et al., 2024). Park, Lee and Kim (2025) keep most of the search index on disk and shorten the passage. That system still aims to answer.
Kamath, Jia and Liang (2020) maximise coverage, the share of questions answered, at a fixed accuracy. They show that a raw top probability is overconfident off the training domain. Semantic entropy is stronger, but it needs several sampled answers (Kuhn, Gal and Farquhar, 2023).
3.2  Critical evaluation of existing systems
Three limits follow. Extra samples are a poor fit on a warm phone, so the score has to come from one answer (Xiao et al., 2024). Four-bit GPTQ quantization moves confidence most on items the model was already unsure about, and calibration error often rises (Proskurina et al., 2024). Repair by soft prompts changes the model (Zhong et al., 2025). This project keeps it frozen. Phone studies treat heat and memory as efficiency, not as a reason to abstain: repeated runs slow under frequency scaling (Xiao et al., 2024), and 4-bit inference uses more energy than 3-bit (Li et al., 2024). MobileRAG still answers, and a fixed threshold cannot see that live state (Park, Lee and Kim, 2025; Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020).
3.3  Proposed methodological justification
The gate therefore reads the single score and raises the confidence required as free memory or thermal headroom falls. A severe thermal state, or the low-memory flag, forces a refusal. The test compares always answering, confidence only, and confidence plus both device signals, at one risk limit.
4.  System Design Architecture
4.1  End-to-end system blueprint
Figure 1 separates the generated answer from the decision to show it. The input is the question and its published passage, so a retrieval miss is not scored as a model error. A live index is out of scope (Section 8). The frozen model returns a candidate and confidence C. The app reads memory, thermal headroom, and latency, then shows the candidate or returns "I'm not sure." Scores are computed off the phone.

Figure 1. Pipeline from a public question and its passage, through a frozen 4-bit model and Android telemetry, to the abstention gate. The heavy border marks the new component. Scores are computed off the phone.
4.2  Architectural sub-systems
Batches are tagged cool or sustained, and normal or reduced memory. There is no network call. llama.cpp runs a 4-bit GGUF file (Xiao et al., 2024; Gerganov and contributors, n.d.). The pilot keeps the larger Qwen2.5 instruction model, at 0.5B or 1.5B parameters, that leaves 1 GB free. Both sizes have been run on a Galaxy S24 (Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). Weights stay frozen. C is the length-normalised token probability of that one answer.
ActivityManager.MemoryInfo supplies available memory and the low-memory flag. PowerManager supplies thermal status and getThermalHeadroom, where 1.0 is the SEVERE threshold (Android Developers, 2026). The forecast argument is 0 seconds, so the reading is the current one. Latency is the monotonic time around generation.
Memory pressure runs from 0 at the idle baseline to 1 when the low-memory flag is set. Thermal pressure is headroom clipped to the range 0 to 1. The app answers only if C ≥ τ, with τ = τ0 + α (memory pressure) + β (thermal pressure), the low-memory flag clear, and thermal status below SEVERE. If headroom is missing, thermal status is mapped from NONE to CRITICAL and used instead. τ0 is the cool-development threshold with the highest coverage at a risk of 20% or below. α and β are fitted afterwards on the warm and low-memory development runs. Confidence-only keeps that τ0 and sets α and β to zero. The test split is not used for fitting. The 20% cap follows Kamath, Jia and Liang (2020). Each log row stores the item id, candidate, C, both pressures, τ, the decision, latency, memory, and headroom.
5.  Applied Methodology and Implementation
5.1  Data engineering and pipeline strategy
SQuAD 2.0 and MuSiQue-Full are used because both already mark items that should be refused (Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, 2018; Trivedi et al., 2022). MuSiQue-Full adds questions of two to four hops. Each set contributes 200 development items and 300 test items, stratified on answerability, with no item in both splits. The only added text is one instruction: answer from the passage, or say that it does not.
A byte buffer lowers free memory without changing the question, so a harder passage is not mistaken for a hardware effect. Passage length is logged, but it is not the memory evidence. The same 300 test items run cool, one at a time after the phone has idled, and warm, back to back for at least ten minutes.
5.2  Model selection and the tuned rule
No weights are trained. Always-answer skips the gate. Ablations set α or β to zero separately, so a failure of memory can be separated from a failure of temperature.
5.3  Technical stack and compute
Kotlin calls the Android APIs and llama.cpp runs the model. Python scores the logs offline, including expected calibration error (Guo et al., 2017). The 50-item pilot records the handset and checks the thermal reading. A second phone is reported as its own tier.
5.4  Quantitative evaluation and expected results
Releasing an unanswerable item is wrong. An answerable item is wrong if token F1 is below 0.5; mean F1 is also reported. Selective risk is the error rate among answered items, and coverage is the fraction answered. The primary result is which method covers more of the test set at a risk of 20% or below. Calibration of C is reported by condition, with latency, memory, and headroom as medians and interquartile ranges, plus the risk–coverage curve.
Support for the gate would be a coverage gain over confidence-only when the phone is warm or short of memory, and little gain when it is cool. No gain would mean the sensors are not justified. If every method exceeds 20% on the test set, the threshold did not transfer. That result will stand, and the coefficients will not be refitted.
6.  Ethical, Legal, and Socio-Economic Considerations
6.1  Regulatory compliance and data governance
Only public research sets are used, under their published licences. No names, contacts, or locations are collected, and prompts stay on the phone, so personal data under the GDPR are not processed. Logs hold item identifiers, outputs, and telemetry.
6.2  Algorithmic bias and mitigation
Both sets are English and largely Wikipedia-style. The thesis will not claim fairness across languages or groups. The error review will note only patterns this design can see, such as multi-hop and unanswerable items.
6.3  Risk, accountability, and explanation
The work is a student prototype, not a high-risk system under the EU AI Act. The refusal is the safety control, and it is checkable because the log stores C, both pressures, and τ. SHAP is not used. The model is frozen, and the decision is already that comparison.

8.  Strengths and Weaknesses
Retrieval is held fixed, τ0 is locked before α and β are fitted, and the test split is used once, so a negative result remains a valid answer. The memory buffer keeps each question's wording constant, and every refusal can be rebuilt from C and τ.
One phone cannot represent every chipset, and frequency policy already differs by vendor (Xiao et al., 2024). Token probability is weaker than semantic entropy, but extra samples would heat the phone and confound the warm run. The passages are not a live retriever, the sets are English only, and 20% is a research choice, not a measured user tolerance.
