1.  Abstract, Introduction and Background
1.1  Context and domain framework
This proposal tests whether a rule fitted on some Android phones can withhold a likely wrong answer on a phone it has not seen. One frozen 4-bit file is copied everywhere. Four scores from one answer join live memory and heat. There is no cloud call. The same 120 SQuAD 2.0 items face always answering, confidence only, and the phone readings only, at most 20% wrong among shown answers. Slow answers are counted separately. A gain that fails on the left-out phone is kept and is not refitted.
1.2  The AI landscape
Handsets that share Android still differ in free memory, speed, and heat (Xiao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025). Refusal methods read only model confidence (Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020), and MobileRAG still answers every time (Park, Lee and Kim, 2025).
1.3  Problem statement
A cutoff from one chip is not evidence about the next, because confidence and the live load differ. Refusing every question answers nothing. Refusing whenever the phone is hot drops correct answers that are only slow.
In the question, (I) is the method, (O) the result, (P) the phones, and (C) the comparison. Coverage is the share of questions that receive an answer. Frozen means the weights are not changed.
Primary research question. To what extent can a device-aware rule (I) hold wrong shown answers to 20% and cut cases that miss a fixed speed limit, while answering as many as possible (O), for one frozen 4-bit model on Android phones that differ in memory and chip (P), compared with always answering, confidence only, and phone readings only (C)?
Sub-RQ 1. How can one answer’s uncertainty be combined with live memory and heat, without training the model again?
Sub-RQ 2. On the same questions, how do wrong answers and slow answers differ across phones, and between a cool run and a long run?
Sub-RQ 3. When one phone is left out, at the 20% limit, does the combined rule answer more than confidence-only, and does that gain shrink when the phone is cool?
2.  Research Aims and Objectives
2.1  Overarching project aim
The aim is to test whether one rule, fitted on some phones, can refuse a likely wrong answer on a phone it has not seen.
2.2  Measurable project objectives
Six objectives follow. (1) Fix the comparisons and the 20% limit before any scored run. (2) Split SQuAD 2.0 and log each phone (Rajpurkar, Jia and Liang, 2018). (3) Build the Section 4 pipeline with no network call. (4) Fit all three rules on the development phones only. (5) Score each left-out phone once. (6) Review 30–60 errors and unnecessary refusals.
3.  Literature Review
3.1  State-of-the-art thematic synthesis
Two lines of work meet here, and neither decides whether to show an answer on an unseen phone. Selective prediction maximises coverage at a chosen error limit, and a raw top probability is over-confident under a question shift (Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020). Qwen2.5 at 0.5B and 1.5B has been run on a Galaxy S24 and still answers (Liu et al., 2024; Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). Longer prompts cut speed by over 10%, and a Snapdragon clock nearly halves by round nine (Xiao et al., 2024). Four-bit inference can use more energy than 3-bit, and the hottest phone differs by test (Li et al., 2025).
3.2  Critical evaluation of existing systems
Semantic entropy needs several answers, which would warm the phone, so this study uses one (Kuhn, Gal and Farquhar, 2023). Four-bit GPTQ shifts confidence where the model was already unsure (Proskurina et al., 2024). Soft-prompt repair changes the model, is hard to inspect after compression, and needs new labels (Zhong et al., 2025). This rule stays outside the frozen weights and uses the answers SQuAD already publishes. A cutoff from one chip is not evidence about the next.
3.3  Proposed methodological justification
The rule reads one answer’s uncertainty with live memory and heat, and it refuses on a low-memory flag or severe heat. The test leaves out a whole phone and compares the combined rule with confidence-only and with phone-readings-only at the same 20% limit. Always answering has no refusal.

4.1  End-to-end system blueprint

4.2  Architectural sub-systems
llama.cpp runs one 4-bit file, with no network call (Gerganov, n.d.). The pilot keeps the larger of Qwen2.5 at 0.5B or 1.5B that leaves 1 GB free on the weakest phone, then copies that file. A Galaxy S24 result does not show that 1.5B fits 4 GB (Park, Lee and Kim, 2025). Features are length-adjusted probability, mean entropy, the lowest probability, and the smallest top-two gap.
getThermalHeadroom(0) is the heat now; 1.0 is SEVERE (Android Developers, 2026). Memory pressure runs from 0 at idle to 1 when memory is low. Phone name, raw megabytes, and battery percentage are logged, not used. The answer is shown only if the estimated chance it is wrong is at or below τ, memory is not low, and heat is below SEVERE. Confidence-only and phone-only share that hard refusal. Always-answer does not. Weights and τ come from the other phones only.
5.  Applied Methodology and Implementation
5.1  Data engineering and pipeline strategy
Every phone sees the same 120 development and 120 test SQuAD items, stratified on answerability, with none shared. There is no second dataset. The reading list is frozen first, from the ACL Anthology, PMLR, and arXiv (2017–2025).
Six phones are planned, two near 4 GB, two at 6–8 GB, and two near 12 GB. The rule is fitted without one phone and scored only on that phone. Fewer than four phones keeps only cool versus long runs. Test items run for at least ten minutes, then the same 40 are repeated after a rest. A buffer stresses 40 development items.
5.2  Model selection and the tuned rule
The language model is not trained. Comparisons are always-answer, confidence-only, and phone-only. Fitting uses the other phones’ development rows, including cool and buffer repeats, and scales from those rows only. An inner split picks the penalty from 0.01, 0.1, 1, and 10, and picks τ for the most coverage at or below 20% answer-error. The left-out phone is scored once. A pooled split is reported only as an optimistic ceiling. A wrong answer is an unanswerable item shown, or token F1 below 0.5. A slow answer exceeds the 90th percentile of cool times on the highest-RAM phone. Training uses wrong answers only (Kamath, Jia and Liang, 2020).
5.3  Technical stack and compute
Kotlin, llama.cpp, and scikit-learn are used, with no cloud GPU. Scoring includes expected calibration error (ECE) (Guo et al., 2017). A 30-question pilot fixes the model size, the speed limit, and a safe buffer.
5.4  Quantitative evaluation and expected results
The headline is mean coverage on the long runs at or below 20% wrong, plus the slow-answer rate and the risk–coverage curve. The buffer run is a stress test, not the headline. A gain over confidence-only on long runs, smaller when cool, would support the rule. If phone-only matches the combined rule, the uncertainty features are unused. No gain, or a rate above 20%, is kept and is not refitted.
6.  Ethical, Legal, and Socio-Economic Considerations
6.1  Regulatory compliance and data governance
Only the public SQuAD 2.0 release is used. Borrowed phones run the test app with no personal account, so the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is not triggered. The prototype is not a high-risk system under the EU AI Act.
6.2  Algorithmic bias and mitigation
The questions are English and Wikipedia-style, so no fairness claim is made. The audit splits error rate and refusal rate by answerability and by whether the refusal follows the long run.
6.3  Risk, accountability, and explanation
SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) are not used: the regression weights already state the chance of a wrong answer. A refusal can spare a paid cloud call and also withholds correct answers. That trade-off is reported as coverage lost on each phone.

8.  Strengths and Weaknesses
The left-out phone never sets the weights or τ, and the test questions are scored once. A negative result stands. Raw RAM cannot name the phone, and every refusing rule shares the hard stop. Six phones will not represent the market (Xiao et al., 2024). A tree on a few hundred rows would fit the training phones, so the weighted sum is the model this sample can support. Passages are supplied, the questions are English only, and 20% is a research choice.
